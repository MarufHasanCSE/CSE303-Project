🔍 Key Findings & Insights

Finding #1: Weight Features Dominate

Physical weight measurements (shell weight, shucked weight, whole weight) are the strongest predictors of age for both species, accounting for over 80% of predictive power.

Finding #2: Crab Data Shows Better Patterns

Crab age prediction models outperformed abalone models with an R² of 0.6234 vs 0.5298, suggesting more linear relationship between physical measurements and age in crabs.

Finding #3: Random Forest Performs Best

The Random Forest algorithm achieved the highest performance across both datasets, capturing non-linear relationships that linear models missed.

Finding #4: Linear Relationships Exist

Despite model complexity, polynomial and linear regressions maintained competitive performance, indicating substantial linear trends in both datasets.

Finding #5: Cross-Species Variations

While weight features are important in both species, crabs show higher importance for carapace width, while abalones emphasize shell weight more strongly.

Finding #6: Prediction Limitations

Even with optimized models, prediction errors range from 3-5 years on average, suggesting age estimation from physical measurements alone has inherent biological variability.

💡 Recommendations

For Fisheries Management

  • Use weight-based models as primary screening tool for age estimation
  • Implement ensemble methods for critical decision-making
  • Consider species-specific models for better accuracy

For Further Research

  • Collect additional features (genetic, environmental factors)
  • Explore deep learning approaches with larger datasets
  • Investigate temporal variations in growth patterns

For Model Improvement

  • Increase training data sample size
  • Add feature engineering with interaction terms
  • Implement cross-validation with larger fold numbers
